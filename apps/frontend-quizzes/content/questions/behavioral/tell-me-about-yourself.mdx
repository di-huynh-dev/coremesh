# Behavioral: Tell Me About Yourself

The opening prompt of almost every interview:

> "Tell me about yourself / Walk me through your resume."

While this seems casual, interviewers use it to establish your technical narrative, scope of ownership, and communication clarity within the first five minutes.

---

## 1. What Interviewers Are Evaluating

- **Communication Clarity**: Can you summarize a complex career path concisely?
- **Scope of Ownership**: Are you a code monkey executing tasks, or a product-minded engineer driving initiatives?
- **Technical Narrative**: Does your stack align with the role?
- **Relevance**: Do you highlight achievements that solve the team's current problems?

---

## 2. The 3-Step Elevator Pitch Formula

A high-impact frontend elevator pitch should follow a **Present-Past-Future** structure, taking no more than 2 minutes.

### 1. Present (The Hook)
State your current role, your core technical stack, and your immediate scope of impact.

*Example*:
> "I'm currently a Senior Frontend Engineer at [Company], where I lead a team of 4 developers responsible for our core e-commerce checkout experience. My daily stack revolves around React, Next.js, and TypeScript, with a strong emphasis on performance optimization and design system implementation."

### 2. Past (The Proof)
Highlight 1 or 2 specific, quantifiable achievements that demonstrate senior leadership, problem-solving, or business impact.

*Example*:
> "Before this, I led the migration of our legacy client-side dashboard to a server-rendered Next.js architecture. By implementing route-based code-splitting, request caching, and optimizing image delivery, we reduced our Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) by 40% and improved our SEO ranking, resulting in a 12% lift in conversion rates. I also established our frontend testing guidelines, scaling unit test coverage from 20% to 80% using Jest and React Testing Library."

### 3. Future (The Connection)
Explain why you are here today, highlighting how your experience aligns with their specific team challenges.

*Example*:
> "While I've loved scaling our checkout application, I'm looking for my next challenge. I'm really excited about your opening because I know you're scaling your SaaS platform and modularizing your frontend codebase. I'm looking to apply my experience in web performance and design systems to help your team ship faster and build a premium user experience."

---

## Senior-Level Interview Answer

A senior engineer’s pitch doesn't just list technologies; it connects technical choices to business outcomes and team growth.

### Core Strengths to Weave In:
- **Product Empathy**: Mention how your code changes improved business metrics (e.g., conversion, user retention).
- **Engineering Excellence**: Highlight mentoring, standardizing code quality, or improving developer velocity.
- **Architectural Ownership**: Focus on systems design, migration strategies, or infrastructure improvements.

---

## Common Interview Mistakes

### ❌ Reciting your resume chronologically
Do not list every job, stack, and minor project since university. The interviewer has your resume; they want a curated highlight reel.

### ❌ Speaking for too long
Keep it under 2 minutes. Focus on interactive dialogue. If you speak for 5+ minutes, you lose the interviewer's attention.

### ❌ Lacking frontend specificity
Avoid generic phrases like "I do full-stack web development." Position yourself as an expert in frontend engineering (e.g., state management, web performance, component architecture).

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Present-Past-Future**: Structure your pitch clearly to keep the timeline coherent.
- **Quantify Impact**: Use numbers (e.g., 40% LCP reduction, 12% lift in conversions) to substantiate your achievements.
- **Align with the Role**: Tailor your "Future" statement to match the specific technology or product challenges of the hiring company.
